The First Church of Gravy

“Blessed are the drippings, for they shall be poured.”

We are a fellowship of the sauced, gathered in the conviction that nothing dry shall be served, and no roast shall want for covering. All boats welcome.

The Creed

What We Believe

We believe in one Sauce, warm and abundant, poured over all things visible and edible.

We believe in the Roux — equal parts fat and flour, patiently browned — by whom all thickening is made.

We believe in the Whisk, breaker of lumps, and in the Simmer everlasting.

We believe the boat shall be passed to the left; that seconds are a right and thirds a calling; and that no potato shall be left unmashed, nor mash unsauced.

This we hold, until the pan is deglazed. Rou‑men.

Scripture

From the Holy Texts

The Book of Roux

Chapter 1, verses 1–7 · The Stovetop Translation

1In the beginning there was the pan, and the pan was hot, and the drippings moved upon the face of the pan.

2And a voice said, let there be flour; and there was flour, and it was sifted.

3And the fat and the flour were joined, and the joining was called Roux; and it was golden, and it was good.

4Then came the stock, a little at a time; for whosoever dumpeth it all at once shall know lumps all the days of their life.

5And the whisk moved without ceasing, and the sauce thickened, and it coated the back of the spoon.

6And it was seasoned, and tasted, and seasoned again.

7And there was simmer, and there was reduction — the first supper.

The Law

The Five Ladlements

  1. I
    Thou shalt not serve it dry. This is the whole of the law. The rest is commentary and cornstarch.
  2. II
    Honor thy drippings. Strain them if thou must, but discard them never, for they are the soul of the pan.
  3. III
    Whisk without ceasing, that lumps may not come to pass among you.
  4. IV
    Remember the Sunday Roast, and keep it moist. Low and slow, from midmorning; the household shall gather at the smell of it.
  5. V
    Covet not thy neighbor’s boat. Pass it to the left, for gravy shared is gravy multiplied.

Rites of the Church

The Four Sacraments

Initiation

The First Pour

By ladle are new congregants welcomed: one generous pour over the mash of initiation, witnessed by at least two elders and a dinner roll.

Communion

The Biscuit

Taken warm, split, and thoroughly sauced. The biscuit is merely the vessel. Observed on Sundays, and honestly most other days.

Confirmation

The Rite of Thickening

At the age of reason, each member prepares their first unsupervised roux, from blond unto brown, without burning. Many are stirred, but few are curdled.

Last Rites

The Final Deglaze

When the meal ends, the pan is honored with wine or stock, and nothing of the fond is lost. So may we all be scraped lovingly from the pan of this world.

Church History

The Great Schism

The Smoothist Communion

Orthodoxy of the Strainer

Holds that gravy, like mercy, must be strained: velvet, unbroken, without blemish or lump. Their vestments are sieves. Their patience is legendary.

The Lumpist Orthodoxy

Old Believers of the Spoon

Holds that the lump is proof of the honest hand, and that a homemade gravy without texture is a store-bought soul. They stir counterclockwise, out of principle.

The two communions were reconciled — mostly — at the Council of Whisk, which ruled that both are canon, and that anyone using granules from a jar should simply not bring it up at the table.

Doctrine, Clarified

Heresies & Frequent Questions

Is plant-based gravy permitted?
Fully canon. The Sauce takes many forms — mushroom, onion, miso. All boats rise.
What of instant granules?
We do not speak of the Granular Heresy. (If pressed: forgiven, when stirred with love and never confessed at the table.)
White gravy or brown?
Yes. Sausage gravy is recognized as a full member of the communion of sauces, with all rights and privileges pertaining thereto.
Is ketchup a gravy?
This question cost us three congregations. The Council has tabled it indefinitely.
What happens when the boat runs out?
Nothing “happens.” But it is customary to sop the boat with bread, that no drop be lost, and to whisper, until the next pour.

The Liturgical Year

Holy Days & Observances

Every Sunday
Sabbath of the Roast. Low and slow, from midmorning. Attendance is taken by aroma.
Fourth Thursday of November
The High Pour. Highest of holy days. Boats are refilled without being asked, and the ladle never touches the table.
The Day After
Feast of the Leftovers. The gravy, reheated, is holier still. This is settled doctrine.
Any Rainy Tuesday
Biscuit Vespers. A quiet service. Bring your own vessel.

Holy Orders

Take Your Vows

The Church ordains all who ask. Enter your name — or don’t; the Sauce knows you already — and receive your title in the Order of the Eternal Simmer.

By the steam and by the stock, be it known that

Vows are binding until dishes are done. Reordination is permitted, encouraged, and frankly expected.

The First Church of Gravy is a non-prophet organization. Services are held wherever two or more are gathered around mashed potatoes.
